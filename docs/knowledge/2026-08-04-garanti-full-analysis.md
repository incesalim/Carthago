# Garanti BBVA — full credit analysis, 2026Q1 (+1H26 external)

> **Status: analysis, report only.** Consolidates and supersedes the two earlier
> Garanti write-ups of 2026-08-04. Structured on the standard Turkish-bank
> framework. Internal figures **unconsolidated, thousand TL**, from
> `data/bank_audit.db` (snapshot 2026-07-27); 1H26 figures external and
> consolidated. CPI y/y 2026-03: **30.87%**.

---

## Recommendation

**Constructive on credit, cautious on capacity.** Garanti is the best-capitalised
large Turkish bank, has the cleanest accounting of the private majors, and has just
posted the sector's clearest funding-cost win. Against that: it took a bigger
regulatory capital hit than the sector, its NPL ratio understates formation by
28bp of disposals, and organic capital generation no longer covers organic RWA
growth.

Senior unsecured to 2 years: comfortable. Beyond that, the constraint is not
solvency — it is whether growth must be curtailed or capital raised in 2027–28.

**Earnings are not purely domestic-bank earnings.** Equity-accounted income is

International N.V.** in the Netherlands — means a fifth of the bottom line is
earned outside the bank and outside Turkish banking regulation. A reader modelling
Garanti as a pure Turkish lender is modelling 80% of it.

## 2. Macro and regulatory

Policy rate **37%**; overnight corridor 35.5% / 40.0%; CBRT effective funding cost
40.0% (MPC ANO2026-17). Consensus is for limited easing: Goldman sees **35% by
end-2026** (~200bp), Fitch no more than 200bp, and Goldman has cut its sector ROE
forecast to **27% from 30%**.

**The regulatory event that matters happened on 1 January 2026: the
fixed-exchange-rate regulatory relief ended.** Fitch estimated in advance that
withdrawal would cut reported capital ratios by **170–200bp** sector-wide. Its
effect on Garanti is quantified in §7 and it is the single most important number
in this file.

⚠️ **Our regulatory data is stale.** Latest briefing 2026-06-07, latest news
2026-06-12, BDDK monthly April 2026. A CBRT macroprudential change of **1 July 2026**
(ANO2026-26) altering FX-deposit reserve requirements is **not** in our data and is
not reflected below. Cause is the cron freeze, not the pipeline.

## 3. Earnings quality

### 3.1 The margin, decomposed

This is the centre of the file. Annualised, on average balances:

| | Q1-25 | Q1-26 | move |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Loan yield** | 28.35% | 25.35% | **−300bp** |
| **Deposit cost** | 22.92% | 18.26% | **−466bp** |
| **Spread** | 5.43pp | **7.09pp** | **+166bp** |

**The entire margin story is that deposit costs fell 166bp faster than loan
yields.** Net interest income doubled — ₺30.4bn → ₺60.8bn — while interest expense
rose just 2.4% on a book that grew 32%. NIM on average assets: 3.84% → 4.31% →
**6.21%**.

Composition of the ₺194.2bn interest income line:

| source | Q1-25 | Q1-26 | share |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Loans | 115.8 | **149.5** | 77.0% |
| Reserve deposits (CBRT remuneration) | 16.2 | 18.5 | 9.5% |
| Securities portfolio | 19.2 | 18.9 | 9.7% |
| Banks + money market | 9.2 | 6.9 | 3.5% |

⚠️ **Two quality flags inside that.** First, **9.5% of interest income is CBRT
remuneration on required reserves** — a policy-set price, not a franchise return,
and it rose 14.5% y/y. Second, the securities portfolio produced *less* income than
a year ago (₺19.2bn → ₺18.9bn) despite a larger balance sheet, with
amortised-cost income down 15% — the securities book is **dilutive** to margin at
an implied ~18% yield against a 25.35% loan yield.

**Funding mix shifted, and cheapened.** Money-market interest expense fell 48%
(₺10.7bn → ₺5.6bn) while interest on securities issued rose 143% (₺1.9bn →
₺4.6bn). Garanti termed out short-term funding into issued paper as rates fell —
good risk management, and it locks in some of the funding gain.

### 3.2 Fees — the cleanest line in the file

Net fees and commissions ₺41.6bn, **+40% y/y**, **35.7% of operating income**,
+39% y/y and +12% q/q on the 1H26 disclosure. Driven by payment systems, money
transfers, insurance and asset management. Against ~31% inflation this is real
growth, not repricing, and it carries no accounting noise.

### 3.3 Costs — the clear negative trend

**Cost/income has deteriorated three years running: 33.6% → 38.5% → 41.8%.**
Personnel +47.5% y/y, other operating expenses **+64.8%** — roughly double
inflation. Management describes a sector-leading cost/income ratio; both can be
true, but the direction is wrong and it is the question IR will be asked.

Effective tax rate rose 15.6% → 21.6% → **24.2%**, removing a prior tailwind.
Trading swung from +₺5.1bn to **−₺4.8bn**, a ₺9.9bn negative swing that NII
absorbed silently.

### 3.4 Accounting quality — the Turkish-specific item

EY qualified Garanti **every quarter from 2022Q1 to 2024Q4** over a free provision
that "does not meet the recognition criteria of TAS 37." It peaked at ₺8.0bn and
was released through 2023 — ₺2.0bn in Q2, ₺5.0bn in Q3, the balance at year-end.

**That ₺8.0bn release is 9.2% of 2023's ₺87.3bn reported profit**, so any
2023-based growth comparison is inflated.

**Clean since 2025Q1.** At 2026Q1, among the large banks, Garanti, Akbank, İş, YKB,
Halk and Ziraat are clean while **Vakıf, QNB Finans, Deniz and TEB remain
qualified**. Garanti is now on the right side of that line, and recently enough
that most screens still carry the old flag.

## 4. Asset quality

| | 2024Q1 | 2024Q4 | 2025Q4 | **2026Q1** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| NPL ratio | 1.89% | 2.24% | 3.52% | **3.69%** |
| Stage 2 share | 10.6% | 12.4% | 11.4% | **12.4%** |
| Stage 3 coverage | 65.2% | 66.7% | 62.4% | **62.9%** |

Sector at April 2026: NPL **2.65%**, coverage **76.3%**. Garanti is 104bp worse on
NPL and 13.4pp worse on coverage. Its **Stage 2 at 12.4% is the highest of the
large banks** (İş 12.0, YKB 12.0, Vakıf 9.9, Halk 8.6, Akbank 8.4, Ziraat 7.7).

### ⚠️ The headline ratio understates formation

`npl_movement`, Q1-26, ₺bn:

| | opening | additions | collections | write-offs | **sold** | closing | coverage |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Group III | 21.82 | **21.55** | 3.11 | 0 | 0.00 | 19.75 | 53.9% |
| Group IV | 30.55 | 0.40 | 2.44 | 0 | **1.45** | 35.16 | 59.9% |
| Group V | 27.87 | 0.66 | 0.53 | **1.04** | **4.47** | 34.96 | 71.0% |
| **total** | **80.24** | **22.61** | **6.08** | **1.04** | **5.93** | **89.86** | **62.9%** |

**₺5.93bn of NPL was sold and ₺1.04bn written off in a single quarter.**
Adding them back gives ₺96.83bn, an NPL ratio of **3.97% against the reported
3.69% — 28bp of the ratio is disposal.**

Gross formation of **₺22.6bn** annualises to **3.71% of loans**, against reported
NPL growth of ₺9.6bn. Group III additions (₺21.55bn) nearly equal its entire
opening balance: the substandard bucket turned over inside one quarter.

Coverage by severity — 53.9% / 59.9% / 71.0% — shows the low headline coverage is
partly a **mix** effect: the book is weighted toward the lighter-provisioned early
buckets, which is what fast formation produces.

### Concentration

`loans_by_sector` (annual lane, 2025Q4): the "Others" bucket — retail — is **60% of
Stage 2 and 63% of Stage 3**. That corroborates the 1H26 disclosure that consumer
loans and cards are **70% of new NPL inflows** and 54% of Stage 3 balances.
Construction carries ₺5.83bn Stage 3 at 70% coverage; manufacturing ₺56.9bn Stage 2.

**This is a consumer-credit deterioration, not a corporate one** — which matters,
because consumer books season faster and are less collateralised.

Cost of risk: **2.3% in 1H26, up from 1.5%**, guidance 2–2.5% "trending toward the
upper end" on restructuring migrations into Stage 3. Management's defence is that
**84% of the Stage 2 SICR portfolio is non-delinquent**.

## 5. Currency risk

**Bank level: minimal.** Net FX position **+₺38.1bn long**, 8.4% of equity — EUR
long ₺48.2bn, USD short ₺10.1bn, on-balance −₺145.2bn offset by +₺183.4bn
off-balance. Management discloses CAR sensitivity to a 10% lira depreciation of
**14.5bp**. Both sources agree: this is a hedged book.

**Structural: a large FX funding surplus.** Deposits are **TL 62.9% / FX 37.1%**;
loans are **TL 74.8% / FX 25.2%**. FX deposits (₺1,005.6bn) exceed FX loans
(₺613.8bn) by **₺391.8bn**. That surplus is deployed through swaps and FX
securities — including a ₺135.6bn FVOCI "other financial assets" book that is
**100% FX**.

⚠️ **Borrower FX risk is not assessable from our data.** Whether the ₺613.8bn of FX
loans sits with exporters who earn foreign currency, or with domestic borrowers who
do not, is the question that turns a hedged bank into an exposed one — and no
extracted lane answers it.

## 6. Funding and liquidity

Deposits are **67.5% of the balance sheet** — a customer-funded profile, not a
wholesale one. LDR 89.9%, below the 91.1% sector.

| | 2024Q1 | 2025Q1 | 2025Q4 | **2026Q1** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| LCR | 232.3 | 147.1 | 149.7 | **144.9** |
| NSFR | 154.4 | 140.7 | 132.7 | **127.3** |
| Leverage ratio | 7.14 | 6.56 | 5.96 | **5.71** |

All comfortably above requirement, but **all three have declined steadily for two
years**. Liquidity has been spent funding growth. There is no room for another
surge without wholesale issuance — and the 143% rise in interest on securities
issued shows that issuance has already begun.

Subordinated debt ₺140.1bn; sub-debt amortisation cost 13bp of capital in Q2-26 — a
known, slow drag and a reason to expect refinancing supply.

⚠️ Not available from our lanes: deposit concentration, average deposit maturity,
retail-vs-corporate split, and the external debt maturity schedule. Sector external
debt is ~USD 178bn (~15.8% of liabilities) with long-term rollover above 100%, but
the per-bank figure is what matters and we do not hold it.

## 7. Capital — and the forbearance unwind

| | 2025Q3 | 2025Q4 | **2026Q1** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| CET1 capital | ₺405.6bn | ₺439.4bn | **₺444.1bn** |
| Total capital | ₺510.3bn | ₺578.2bn | ₺591.8bn |
| RWA | ₺2,507.6bn | ₺2,645.6bn | **₺3,154.8bn** |
| CET1 ratio | 16.18% | 16.61% | **14.08%** |
| CAR | 20.35% | 21.85% | 18.76% |
| **RWA / assets** | 71.7% | 69.2% | **78.5%** |

**CET1 capital rose 1.1%. RWA rose 19.2% against 5.1% asset growth.** Risk density
stepped **9.3pp** after eighteen months in a stable 69–72% band, and the entire
253bp CET1 decline is denominator.

**The cause is the fixed-FX regulatory relief ending on 1 January 2026** — the first
day of that quarter. Against Fitch's 170–200bp sector estimate, **Garanti took
253bp: more than the sector average.** Its ratio was propped, by more than most,
and the prop is now gone.

For a credit reader this cuts two ways. The bad news is the level. **The good news
is that what remains is unforborne and disclosed** — and anyone screening Garanti
on a pre-January capital ratio is reading a number that no longer exists.

**The forward problem, from the Q2-26 capital walk:** market and credit risk growth
**−92bp**, sub-debt amortisation −13bp, FX −6bp, against internal capital
generation of **+75bp**. **Organic generation no longer covers organic RWA growth.**
Consolidated CAR is 15.9% against a 12.16% requirement with **₺149bn of excess
capital** — at −36bp net per quarter that funds roughly two to three years before
the choice is issuance or slower growth.

Dividend: ₺18.4bn paid in 2025Q4 from ₺92.2bn of 2024 profit — a **20% payout**,
conservative, but a live question if the above holds.

Quality of capital is good: the CAR−CET1 gap is **4.68pp**, so non-core is ~25% of
regulatory capital — modest for the market. CET1 of 14.08% is the **highest of the
large banks**.

## 8. Securities and sovereign exposure

Government securities **₺412.1bn — 10.3% of assets**, low for a Turkish bank and
the reason Garanti's margin is a deposit story rather than a linker story.

| classification | amount | share |
|---|--:|--:|
| FVTPL | ₺13.4bn | 3.2% |
| FVOCI | ₺154.7bn | 37.5% |
| **Amortised cost** | **₺243.9bn** | **59.2%** |

⚠️ **59.2% of the sovereign book is at amortised cost** — carried at par, with no
valuation flowing through equity or P&L. Any rate-driven loss on that portion is
invisible in reported capital. With the policy rate at 37% and only ~200bp of cuts
expected, this is a latent rather than crystallising risk, but it is unquantifiable
from our data because duration is not extracted.

Separately, FVOCI "other financial assets" of **₺135.6bn is 100% FX** — a
significant non-government FX securities book that does mark through equity.

Management disclosed that using a 30% CPI assumption instead of 27% would have
added **~₺2bn** to Q2 net income, so linker sensitivity is real but second-order.

## 10. Governance

BBVA at 85.97% is a committed, operationally involved parent — a credit positive
that also means minority holders are along for the ride. Payout 20%.

**Guidance accuracy is the concern.** Q2-26 EPS missed consensus by **15.7%**, and
this followed misses in Q3 and Q4 2025 — three of the last four quarters. Full-year
guidance was kept "broadly intact" and the +75bp margin expansion target described
as "achievable but faces risks", with management revising its CPI assumption to 30%
from 23%. A reader should discount management's margin guidance accordingly.

⚠️ Related-party lending — a standard rating-agency question — is not extracted and
is not assessed here.

## 11. Valuation — structurally unavailable

**No valuation is offered.** The BIST price lane was removed on 2026-08-01 because
Yahoo's terms forbid redistribution, so P/B, P/TB, P/E and dividend yield cannot be
computed or published from our data. This is a licensing constraint, not a gap in
the analysis.

What can be said without a price: **ROE 29.3% TTM against 30.87% CPI is −1.2% in
real terms.** On the residual-income logic that drives justified P/B, a bank
earning below inflation is not creating value in real terms regardless of how the
nominal ROE screens. The 1H26 reported ROE of 28% says the same thing.

## Scenarios

**Base — gradual disinflation, ~200bp of cuts.** Spread compresses from 7.09pp as
loan yields keep falling and deposit costs approach a floor; fees carry growth;
cost of risk settles at the 2.5% top of guidance; RWA growth moderates with credit
demand. CET1 drifts toward 13%. **Manageable.**

**Adverse — sticky inflation, no cuts, funding competition intensifies.** The
deposit-cost win reverses first: Garanti's advantage was 466bp of falling deposit
cost, and that is the most competitive price in the market. NII growth stalls;
cost/income breaks 45%; consumer NPL formation continues at 3.7%+ and coverage
falls below 60%. CET1 to ~12% and the growth-versus-issuance decision arrives in
2027. **This is the case management's own commentary describes.**

**Severe — sharp lira depreciation plus deposit flight.** Direct FX damage is
limited (14.5bp per 10%), but three second-order channels bite: FX-denominated RWA
inflates against lira capital; the ₺243.9bn amortised-cost sovereign book takes
unrecognised losses that become real if it must be sold for liquidity; and
unhedged FX borrowers within the ₺613.8bn FX loan book default. **The third is the
one we cannot size, and it is the one that matters.**
