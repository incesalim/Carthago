# Garanti BBVA (GARAN) — credit analysis, 2026Q1

> **Status: analysis, report only.** No code or data changed. Figures from
> `data/bank_audit.db` (snapshot 2026-07-27) and `data/bddk_data.db`, unconsolidated
> unless stated, **thousand TL** unless stated. External anchors cited inline.
> Sector figures are BDDK monthly, April 2026. CPI y/y at 2026-03: **30.87%**.

---

## Bottom line

**Garanti earns its premium on the liability side and is spending it on the asset
side.** The quarter's headline — net profit ₺33.3bn, +32% y/y, ROE 29.3% — is real
and driven by a genuine funding-cost win: net interest income **doubled** while
interest expense rose 2.4%. But three things move the other way at once, and two of
them are not visible in the headline ratios:

1. **RWA grew 19.2% in a single quarter against 5.1% asset growth**, taking CET1
   from 16.61% to 14.08%. Capital did not erode — the denominator exploded.
2. **NPL has nearly doubled in two years** (1.89% → 3.69%) with Stage 2 at 12.4%,
   the highest of the large private banks, and coverage at 62.9% against a sector
   76.3%.
3. **ROE of 29.3% is −1.2% in real terms.** The bank is not compounding capital.

The credit view: **strong bank, deteriorating collateral position, one unexplained
capital step.** Comfortable for senior unsecured to 2 years; the RWA step and the
Stage 2 build are what to underwrite before going longer.

## 2. The engine — where the money actually came from

Q1, unconsolidated, ₺bn:

| | Q1-24 | Q1-25 | Q1-26 | y/y |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Interest income | 96.7 | 160.6 | 194.2 | +20.9% |
| Interest expense | 77.1 | 130.2 | 133.4 | **+2.4%** |
| **Net interest income** | 19.7 | 30.4 | **60.8** | **+99.9%** |
| Net fees & commissions | 18.7 | 29.7 | 41.6 | +40.0% |
| Net trading | +4.9 | +5.1 | **−4.8** | — |
| Total operating profit | 55.6 | 79.9 | 116.4 | +45.7% |
| ECL (−) | 14.9 | 22.5 | 30.2 | +34.3% |
| Personnel (−) | 7.4 | 11.9 | 17.6 | +47.5% |
| Other opex (−) | 11.3 | 18.8 | 31.0 | +64.8% |
| Equity-method income | 4.5 | 5.8 | 8.8 | +52.1% |
| **Net profit** | 22.3 | 25.3 | **33.3** | **+31.8%** |

**The whole story is in the second row.** Interest expense grew 2.4% while the book
grew 32%. That is the CBRT easing cycle transmitting through deposit costs faster
than through loan yields — exactly what BBVA Research and the sector commentary
describe as "wider TL customer spread as lower TL deposit costs more than offset
declining loan yields." NIM on average assets went **3.84% → 4.31% → 6.21%**.

**But the quality of that beat is mixed:**

- Trading swung from +₺5.1bn to **−₺4.8bn**, a ₺9.9bn negative swing that NII
  absorbed without difficulty. In a worse NII quarter it would have been visible.
- **Cost/income has deteriorated three years running: 33.6% → 38.5% → 41.8%.** Other
  operating expenses grew 64.8% y/y, roughly double inflation. This is the clearest
  negative operating trend in the filing.
- Effective tax rate rose 15.6% → 21.6% → **24.2%**, removing a prior tailwind.

**Cross-check:** the consolidated net profit our extraction produces for 2026Q1 is
**₺33,615,247 thousand**, matching the ₺33,615m the bank reported in its own 1Q26
release exactly.

## 3. Capital — the step nobody would see

| | 2025Q1 | 2025Q2 | 2025Q3 | 2025Q4 | **2026Q1** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| CET1 capital (₺bn) | 336.6 | 373.3 | 405.6 | 439.4 | **444.1** |
| Total capital (₺bn) | 415.8 | 452.9 | 510.3 | 578.2 | 591.8 |
| RWA (₺bn) | 2,159.0 | 2,351.1 | 2,507.6 | 2,645.6 | **3,154.8** |
| CET1 % | 15.59 | 15.88 | 16.18 | 16.61 | **14.08** |
| CAR % | 19.26 | 19.26 | 20.35 | 21.85 | 18.76 |
| **RWA / assets** | 71.1% | 72.4% | 71.7% | **69.2%** | **78.5%** |

⚠️ **CET1 capital rose 1.1% in the quarter. RWA rose 19.2%.** Assets grew only 5.1%.
Risk density stepped up **9.3pp in one quarter** after eighteen months in a stable
69–72% band. The entire 2.53pp CET1 decline is denominator.

This is the single most important unexplained item in the filing. Candidate causes
— a mix shift into higher-weight lending, expiry of a BDDK forbearance on risk
weights, or a model change — are not distinguishable from stored data, and the
answer determines whether this is a one-off reset or the first of several steps.
**Underwrite this before extending tenor.**

Set against that, the capital that remains is high quality. The CAR−CET1 gap is
**4.68pp**, so non-core instruments are ~25% of regulatory capital — modest by
Turkish standards. CET1 of 14.08% is the **best of the large banks**: Akbank 11.71,
İş 11.74, YKB 10.81, Ziraat 11.94, Vakıf 9.41, Halk 8.44. Sector CAR is 16.37%
against Garanti's 18.76%.

Dividends of **₺18.4bn** were paid in 2025Q4 out of 2024 net profit of ₺92.2bn — a
**20% payout**, conservative and leaving 80% to compound.

## 4. Asset quality — the trend that matters

| | 2024Q1 | 2024Q4 | 2025Q2 | 2025Q4 | **2026Q1** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| NPL ratio | 1.89% | 2.24% | 3.00% | 3.52% | **3.69%** |
| Stage 2 share | 10.6% | 12.4% | 11.1% | 11.4% | **12.4%** |
| Stage 3 coverage | 65.2% | 66.7% | 65.4% | 62.4% | **62.9%** |
| LCR | 232.3 | 152.2 | 144.6 | 149.7 | **144.9** |
| NSFR | 154.4 | 136.6 | 132.9 | 132.7 | **127.3** |

**NPL has nearly doubled in eight quarters** and sits **104bp above the sector's
2.65%**. Coverage at 62.9% is **13.4pp below the sector's 76.3%**. Cost of risk,
annualising Q1 ECL over the loan book, is **4.95%** — up from 2.45% two years ago.

Against peers at 2026Q1, Garanti's Stage 2 at **12.4% is the highest of the large
banks** (İş 12.0, YKB 12.0, QNB Finans 11.6, TEB 11.1, Deniz 10.4, Vakıf 9.9, Halk
8.6, Akbank 8.4, Ziraat 7.7). Stage 2 is the pipeline; a book carrying an eighth of
its exposure one step from default, with coverage below sector, is the reason to
watch this name rather than the capital ratio.

This is consistent with the external read that credit risk is "rising, particularly
in Retail and SME segments, leading banks to tighten underwriting and increase
provisions." Garanti is not an outlier in direction — it is early and large in
degree.

**Liquidity is comfortable but has been spent.** LCR fell from 232% to 145% in two
years and NSFR from 154% to 127%. Both are well above requirement; neither has room
to fund another growth surge without wholesale issuance.

## 5. Rate positioning — the tension in the file

The repricing ladder at 2026Q1 (₺bn, rate-sensitive assets less liabilities):

| bucket | gap | cumulative |
|---|--:|--:|
| < 1 month | +274.8 | +274.8 |
| 1–3 months | −88.3 | +186.5 |
| 3–12 months | +230.7 | **+417.2** |
| 1–5 years | +510.3 | +927.5 |
| > 5 years | +138.5 | +1,066.0 |
| non-sensitive | −1,070.1 | −4.2 |

**Cumulative 12-month gap is +₺417bn — 10.4% of assets, asset-sensitive.** A bank
positioned this way *loses* net interest income when rates fall.

That sits awkwardly against the last four quarters, where falling rates *doubled*
NII. The reconciliation is that the gain came from **deposit repricing behaviour**
(betas, mix, the CBRT's relaxation of TL deposit rules) rather than from contractual
repricing dates. The static gap says the next leg of easing works against Garanti;
the realised experience says liability betas dominated. **Both can be true, and the
second is the less durable.**

External consensus supports caution: Goldman Sachs sees the policy rate at 35% by
end-2026 (only ~200bp of cuts) and has cut its sector ROE forecast to **27% from
30%**; Fitch projects no more than 200bp. The commentary expects NIM pressure to
become visible after Q2-26 earnings. The policy rate was **37%** at the June 2026
MPC, with the overnight corridor at 35.5%/40.0% and CBRT effective funding cost
40.0%.

**FX is not a risk here.** Net position **+₺38.1bn long**, 8.4% of equity: EUR long
₺48.2bn, USD short ₺10.1bn, on-balance −₺145.2bn offset by +₺183.4bn off-balance.
Standard hedged structure.

Government securities are **₺412bn, 10.3% of assets** — modest, so the CPI-linker
dependence that flatters some Turkish banks' NII is not the main driver here.

## 6. Accounting quality — a bank that cleaned itself up

Garanti carried a free provision of ₺4.8bn (2022Q1) rising to ₺8.0bn, and EY
qualified the opinion **every quarter from 2022Q1 to 2024Q4** on the grounds it
"does not meet the recognition criteria of TAS 37." It was released in stages
through 2023 — ₺2.0bn in Q2, ₺5.0bn in Q3, the balance by year-end.

**That ₺8.0bn release is 9.2% of 2023's ₺87.3bn reported profit**, and any 2023-base
growth comparison is inflated by it.

The opinion has been **clean since 2025Q1**. Among the large banks at 2026Q1,
Garanti, Akbank, İş, YKB, Halk and Ziraat are clean; **Vakıf, QNB Finans, Deniz and
TEB remain qualified**. On accounting posture Garanti now sits on the right side of
the sector's dividing line — a change worth crediting, and recent enough that most
screens still carry the old flag.

## 8. What would change the view

**Bear triggers**
- RWA density steps again in Q2-26 → the Q1 move was structural, and CET1 goes
  through 12% within a year.
- Stage 2 above 14% or coverage below 60% → the pipeline converts and provisioning
  has to catch up out of earnings.
- NII growth below ~20% y/y in Q2-26 → the deposit-beta win has run its course and
  the asset-sensitive gap starts to bite, as the static ladder implies.
- Cost/income above 45%.

**Bull triggers**
- RWA density reverts toward 72% → Q1 was a one-off reclassification.
- Coverage rebuilds toward the 76% sector level while NPL formation flattens.
- Fee income holds above 35% of operating income — it has been the most reliable
  line in the file, +40% y/y with no accounting noise.

**The single number to watch:** RWA/assets at 2026Q2. Everything about the capital
trajectory depends on whether 78.5% is the new level or a one-quarter artefact.

## 9. Gaps in this analysis

Stated rather than hidden:

- **RWA composition is not extracted.** The Pillar-3 §4-X block (RWA by risk type)
  is untaken, so the single most important question here cannot be answered from
  stored data.
- **Personnel count is NULL** for every recent Garanti period, so
  productivity-per-employee comparisons are unavailable.
- **Stage definitions are not extracted**, so the Stage 2 comparison across peers
  assumes definitional consistency that has not been verified.
- **NPL movement** was not used here; it would decompose whether NPL growth is
  formation or seasoning, and it is available.
- Sector ROE of 8.84% is BDDK's 4-month YTD measure and is **not** directly
  comparable to a TTM ROE; the ~26.5% annualised equivalent is used above.
