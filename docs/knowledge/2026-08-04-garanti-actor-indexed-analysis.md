## What the actor lens changes

The first memo answered a question nobody asks: *"how is Garanti doing?"* No actor
in the research asks that. They ask "how far is CET1 above the AT1 trigger", "can
they afford the deposit price they are quoting", "is my ₺500m safe for three
months", "is the subsidiary underperforming or is the market underperforming".

Same bank, same rows, six different memos. Below are the ones Garanti actually has
readers for.

---

## First: spine question 6, answered

The actor research names seven questions a bank CEO asks and flags that two are
**"genuinely missing and both are narrative-side"**. Question 6 is *"whose ratios
are propped up by regulatory relief"* — also the AT1 holder's second question,
*"is the reported ratio calculated using BDDK's forbearance FX rate?"*

**For Garanti the answer is now determinate.**

| | 2025Q4 | 2026Q1 | move |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| CET1 capital | ₺439.4bn | ₺444.1bn | **+1.1%** |
| RWA | ₺2,645.6bn | ₺3,154.8bn | **+19.2%** |
| Total assets | ₺3,820.9bn | ₺4,016.8bn | +5.1% |
| RWA / assets | 69.2% | **78.5%** | **+9.3pp** |
| CET1 ratio | 16.61% | **14.08%** | **−253bp** |

CET1 *capital* rose. The ratio fell entirely on the denominator, and risk density
stepped 9.3pp after eighteen months in a stable 69–72% band.

**The cause is regulatory, not commercial: the fixed-exchange-rate regulatory
relief ended on 1 January 2026** — the first day of the quarter in which this step
appears. Fitch estimated in advance that removing it would cut reported capital
ratios by **170–200bp** across the sector.

**Garanti's hit was 253bp — above the top of Fitch's sector range.** That is the
answer to spine 6 for this bank: its ratio *was* propped, by more than the average,
and the prop is now gone. It is also the answer for anyone who screened Garanti on
capital before January and hasn't re-screened since.

This is worth noting as a capability result too: the question the actor research
called narrative-side and unanswerable was resolved here by stored capital rows
plus one dated external fact. It did not need the narrative lane.

## C2 — BBVA, the parent (85.97%)

*Their questions: is our subsidiary out- or under-performing the local market, or
just the local market? What return are we earning on capital allocated to Türkiye?
When local management says "the market was difficult", is that true? Will we be
required to inject capital? Can we get dividends out?*

**Is it the bank or the market?** It is the market, and Garanti is handling it
better than most. Sector NPL is 2.65% and Garanti is 3.69% — worse. But sector CAR
is 16.37% and Garanti is 18.76%; sector coverage 76.3% against Garanti's 62.9%.
The mixed scorecard resolves on peers: Garanti's **CET1 of 14.08% is the highest of
the large banks** — Akbank 11.71, İş 11.74, Ziraat 11.94, YKB 10.81, Vakıf 9.41,
Halk 8.44. On the metric that constrains growth, the subsidiary leads its market.

**Is "the market was difficult" true?** Yes, and specifically so. Q2-26 net income
fell single-digit against Q1 on lower trading and higher provisions; EPS missed
consensus by **15.7%**; the shares fell 1.9%. But the sector-wide driver is
independently visible: Goldman cut its sector ROE forecast to 27% from 30% and now
sees only ~200bp of easing to a 35% policy rate; Fitch says no more than 200bp.
Management's line — *"the real story is higher cost of funding and maybe more
issues in the sector in terms of competing for deposit costs"* — is corroborated,
not spin.

**Return on capital allocated to Türkiye.** ROE 29.3% TTM on our numbers, 28%
reported for 1H26. Against **30.87% CPI that is −1.2% real.** For a parent
comparing Türkiye with Mexico or Colombia, the honest framing is that the Turkish
franchise is running hard to stand still in real terms — while consuming capital
that must be held in lira.

**Will capital be required?** Not on this evidence. Consolidated CAR 15.9% against
a 12.16% requirement, **excess capital ₺149bn**, and sensitivity to a 10% lira
depreciation of only **14.5bp** — a genuinely well-hedged position, consistent with
the ₺38.1bn net long FX book (8.4% of equity) we hold. The pressure is RWA growth,
not FX.

⚠️ **The one thing to press local management on:** the Q2 capital walk was
−92bp from market and credit risk growth against +75bp of internal generation.
**Organic capital generation no longer covers organic RWA growth.** At that run
rate the ₺149bn buffer funds roughly two to three years of growth before an
issuance or a growth cut is required. That is the question for the next board pack,
and it is not in any headline ratio.

**Dividends.** ₺18.4bn was paid in 2025Q4 out of ₺92.2bn of 2024 profit — a **20%
payout**, leaving 80% to compound. There is no distribution problem; there is a
question about whether 20% is still affordable if RWA keeps outgrowing generation.

## A1 — Credit investors (Eurobond, AT1, Tier 2)

*Their questions: how far is CET1 above the trigger, and what single event moves it
100bp? Is the ratio using the forbearance FX rate? How much capital build was
CPI-linker income that reverses? Distributable reserves for the coupon? Maturity
wall? Lira −20%?*

**Distance to trigger.** Consolidated CET1 is ~**11.9%** at end-June 2026, down
from 13.1% at YE2025. A standard Turkish AT1 trigger is 5.125% CET1, so the buffer
is wide — this is not a conversion story. The live question is the **12.16%
consolidated CAR requirement against 15.9% actual**: 3.74pp, or ₺149bn.

**What moves it 100bp?** Now answerable from the Q2 walk: market and credit risk
growth alone took **92bp in one quarter**. So the answer is "one more quarter of
the same growth", and that has already happened twice.

**Is the ratio propped?** It *was* — see above. It no longer is. **This is the
single most important thing for this reader and it is good news:** the 253bp has
been taken, in the open, and what remains is unforborne. A buyer screening on
pre-2026 ratios is looking at a number that no longer exists; a buyer screening
today is looking at a clean one.

**CPI-linker reversal risk is low here.** Government securities are **₺412bn, 10.3%
of assets** — modest. Garanti's margin story is a deposit-cost story, not a linker
story, which makes it more durable than peers whose NII leans on linkers as
inflation falls. Management's own disclosure cuts the other way and should be
weighted: revising the CPI assumption to 30% from 27% would have **added ~₺2bn** to
Q2 net income, so linker income is a real but second-order lever.

**Lira −20%.** Management discloses 14.5bp of CAR sensitivity to a 10% move; our FX
lane shows net long ₺38.1bn, 8.4% of equity, with on-balance −₺145.2bn offset by
+₺183.4bn off-balance. Both point the same way: **FX is not the risk in this
credit.**

**Subordinated debt** stands at ₺140.1bn, and sub-debt amortisation cost 13bp of
capital in Q2 — a slow, known drag, and a reason to expect refinancing supply.

## A5 — Corporate treasurers

*Their questions: if I place ₺500m here for three months, do I get it back? Which
of my eight banks is weakest? Has anyone moved tier?*

**Yes.** On the metrics that matter for a 90-day placement: LCR 144.9%, NSFR
127.3%, LDR 89.9% (below the 91.1% sector), equity/assets 11.24%, and the best CET1
of the large banks. Deposits are 67.5% of the balance sheet — a funding profile
dominated by customers, not wholesale markets.

**Ranked against the peers a Turkish treasurer actually holds**, on CET1 at 2026Q1:
Garanti 14.08 · Ziraat 11.94 · İş 11.74 · Akbank 11.71 · YKB 10.81 · Vakıf 9.41 ·
**Halk 8.44**. If the policy is "reduce exposure to the weakest", Garanti is not
where the reduction comes from.

⚠️ **One thing a treasurer would want flagged, since ratings lag:** the direction of
travel is negative on both capital (−253bp in a quarter) and asset quality (NPL
1.89% → 3.69% in eight quarters). None of it threatens a 90-day placement. All of
it matters for an annual limit review.

## C1 — Competitor desks (Garanti as the bank being watched)

*Their questions: what is Garanti paying for deposits? Can they afford the price
they are quoting? Are they capital- or growth-constrained and therefore unable to
respond?*

**What they pay.** Not directly observable, but the derived answer is sharper than
a rate sheet: Q1-26 interest expense grew **2.4%** while the book grew 32%.
Garanti repriced its deposit base down faster than it grew it. Management calls
free funds its structural advantage and claims the strongest free-funds position
among private banks — the sector demand-deposit share is 37.8%, and a competitor
should assume Garanti is above it.

**Can they afford their price?** On funding, yes — that is the whole 1H26 story. On
capital, **less than three months ago**. The 253bp CET1 step and −92bp/quarter of
risk-weighted growth against +75bp of generation mean Garanti's headroom for a
share-buying campaign is materially smaller than its 18.76% CAR implies.

⚠️ **The competitive read:** Garanti is currently strong on price and weakening on
capacity. A rival that reads the CAR headline concludes Garanti can outspend it. A
rival that reads the capital walk concludes Garanti has perhaps two to three years
of organic growth before it must choose between growth and issuance. **That gap
between the headline and the walk is the tradeable knowledge**, and it is exactly
what the actor research says is currently sourced from gossip.

## B3 — Garanti's own IR

*Their questions: what will an analyst ask on Thursday that I cannot answer? Will
our cost/income look worse than everyone's, and what is the honest reason?*

**The question they will get asked:** why did RWA grow 19.2% in a quarter when
assets grew 5.1%? "The forbearance ended" is true and sufficient, but it invites
the follow-up — *your 253bp was above Fitch's 170–200bp sector range; why were you
more forborne than peers?* That is the one to have an answer ready for.

**Cost/income.** Management cites a sector-leading cost/income ratio. Our
computation on the unconsolidated P&L shows it **deteriorating three years running:
33.6% → 38.5% → 41.8%**, with other operating expenses +64.8% y/y against ~31%
inflation. Both can be true — leading the sector while worsening — but the trend is
the question, and "inflation accounting" is the honest reason only if it is
quantified.

**The line that will be quoted back.** Fees +39% y/y and +12% q/q is the cleanest
number in the file, ~35.7% of operating income, with no accounting noise anywhere
near it. Lead with it.

## D3 — Regulators

*Their question: which bank is an outlier, and is it gaming a definition?*

Garanti is not gaming; it is disclosing. But two things make it an informative
outlier for BDDK's own purposes:

1. Its forbearance unwind at **253bp** was above the sector estimate, which makes
   it a useful measure of how unevenly the relief was distributed.
2. Its **Stage 2 at 12.4% is the highest of the large banks** while its coverage at
   62.9% is 13.4pp below sector. Management's defence is that **84% of the Stage 2
   SICR portfolio is non-delinquent** — a strong argument that this is
   forward-looking classification rather than hidden impairment, and precisely the
   kind of claim that is unverifiable across banks because stage definitions are
   not comparable.

That second point is the actor research's "comparability questions have no source
at all", instantiated on a live bank.

## What the 1H26 external data adds that our lanes cannot

Our snapshot stops at 2026Q1. The published Q2 changes the trajectory:

- **Q2 net income fell single-digit q/q**, on lower trading and higher provisions —
  the first sequential decline in the run.
- **Cost of risk 2.3% in 1H26, up from 1.5%**, guidance 2–2.5% "trending toward the
  upper end" on restructuring migrations into Stage 3.
- **New NPL formation ₺29.7bn in Q2**; consumer loans and cards are **70% of new
  NPL inflows** and 54% of Stage 3 balances.
- Coverage **63%, unchanged** — so provisioning is keeping pace with formation but
  not rebuilding the buffer.
- Q3 guided "relatively flat", improvement expected in Q4, FY +75bp margin
  expansion "achievable but faces risks". July deposit costs improved 100bp+.
- 2.4m new customers in H1, total 31m.

**The consumer/card concentration is the finding our lanes miss.** `loans_by_sector`
is extracted but was not used here, and would show whether that 70% is a Garanti
mix problem or a sector one.

## The verdict, by reader

| Actor | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **BBVA (parent)** | Franchise is out-performing its market on capital and matching it on earnings, but generating −1.2% real ROE and no longer covering RWA growth organically. Press on the capital walk, not the P&L. |
| **AT1 / Tier 2 holder** | Constructive. Trigger distance is wide, the forbearance prop is gone and disclosed, FX sensitivity is minimal, linker dependence is low. The risk is RWA growth, not solvency. |
| **Corporate treasurer** | Safe for a 90-day placement; strongest CET1 of the large banks. Flag the direction of travel at annual review. |
| **Competitor** | Strong on price, weakening on capacity. The CAR headline overstates their room to fight. |
| **Garanti IR** | Prepare the "why were you more forborne than peers" answer. Lead with fees. |
| **Regulator** | Not gaming — but the highest Stage 2 with below-sector coverage, defended by a non-delinquency claim no outsider can verify. |

## Gaps, stated

- **RWA composition is not extracted** (Pillar-3 §4-X). The forbearance attribution
  above is inferred from timing and magnitude against Fitch's sector estimate, not
  read from Garanti's own disclosure. It should be verified in the filing.
- **`loans_by_sector` and `npl_movement` were not used** and would answer the
  consumer/card concentration and formation-versus-seasoning questions directly.
- **Stage definitions are not extracted**, so every Stage 2 comparison here assumes
  a consistency that has not been verified — the same caveat the regulator section
  is about.
- Our data stops at 2026Q1; everything Q2 is external and consolidated, so it is
  not on the same basis as the internal figures.
